## C. Results

This section presents the results of executing the metamorphic test scenarios against our Digital Twin prototype within the Living Lab. The evaluation consists of executing the defined what-if scenarios (cf. Tab. II) under realistic conditions and monitoring the outcomes of the metamorphic relations. We structure our findings along the three evaluation questions (EQ1–EQ3).

### 1) Reachability and Stability (EQ1)
To determine if all configured actors and sensors are correctly registered, reachable, and exhibit stable behavior over time, we analyzed the outcomes of scenarios S1, S2, S7, S9, and S10. 
Overall, baseline reachability and correctness were successfully established: the monotony (S1) and invariance (S2) scenarios achieved a 100% pass rate (20/20 and 5/5 passes, respectively), confirming proper addressing, lack of duplicate processing, and absence of stale states. Extended monitoring of sensor stability under constant control (S10) further yielded consistent sensor variance within acceptable noise margins (10/10 passes).
However, evaluating temporal behavior and pipeline latency (S7) revealed 5 stability failures out of 10 executions. In these cases, observed sensor fluctuations (e.g., an amplitude of 0.37 lx) exceeded the allowed tolerance threshold (0.112 lx) over the defined 5-second sampling window. This highlights the inherent difficulties in DT synchronization; unpredictable network jitter and delayed actuator settling times can lead to transient instabilities that violate strict behavioral assumptions. Finally, context-aware execution (S9) demonstrated proper integration of live occupancy constraints. Tests enforcing room-absence preconditions correctly skipped execution when occupancy was detected, and live execution successfully validated expected transitions when constraints were met.

### 2) Spatial Interactions and Proportionality (EQ2)
To investigate spatial coupling and check whether illuminance changes are localized to intended workplaces without undue interference, we executed scenarios S3 and S5.
Our evaluation of cross-workplace leakage (S5) exposed significant spatial interaction. The scenario explicitly specified a `not proportionality` relation, anticipating that a luminaire would not proportionally influence a sensor positioned at a different, unassociated workplace. Unexpectedly, all 10 tests for this relation failed—meaning that the relation passed and proportionality *did* exist. This indicates pronounced cross-workplace leakage, demonstrating that physical light propagates predictably to neighboring workspaces and is accurately registered by the DT.
Conversely, when evaluating strict proportionality bounds on directly associated actuator-sensor pairs (S5), we recorded 5 relation failures. While illuminance did scale with set-point adjustments, the deviation in percentage changes between expected and observed values ranged from 11% to 13%, violating our strict 5% tolerance. 
Furthermore, testing the conservation of aggregate illuminance when redistributing set-points among multiple luminaires (S3) resulted in 15 passes and 5 failures. The failures exhibited discrepancies of up to 0.27 lx above the accumulated noise tolerance, exposing non-linear spatial coupling and allocation effects when multiple light cones intersect.

### 3) Hardware Evolution and Replacement (EQ3)
To assess whether the DT preserves device semantics and whether relations remain valid after hardware changes or environmental drift, we analyzed scenarios S6 and S8.
In the simple substitution scenario (S6), an existing sensor was swapped with another of the same type. While the DT's interface rebinding succeeded and 30 tests passed seamlessly, we observed 5 conservation relation failures. The replaced sensor's readings deviated from the baseline expectation by up to 0.38 lx (against an allowed tolerance of 0.127 lx). This reveals that seemingly identical hardware replacements can introduce subtle, uncalibrated hardware offsets, compromising previously valid equality relations unless the DT's semantic bindings are recalibrated.
Finally, we investigated relation consistency across different twilight and daylight contexts (S8). Although relative metamorphic relations proved remarkably robust across different baselines—yielding 119 passes—extreme environmental variations eventually distorted absolute illuminance baselines, causing 21 conservation failures. This underscores the challenge of defining universal tolerances for cyber-physical DTs, as environmental interference can dynamically shift the boundaries of acceptable relation consistency.
